How to Parry in Mistfall Hunter (Greatsword Skill — No Lock-On)

Straight answer: Mistfall Hunter has no universal parry button and no lock-on. "Parry" is a Greatsword skill: you enter a defensive stance, block frontal attacks (negating damage) and recover Energy on each successful parry, then follow up with a branch attack. Here is exactly how it works, how to use it, and how to fight well without lock-on.

Quick answer

  • There is no global parry key. Parry is a weapon skill on the Greatsword, available to every class — skills are slotted on a weapon and shared by all weapons of the same type.
  • Effect: assume a defensive stance that parries frontal attacks (negating damage) and recovers Energy on each successful parry.
  • Duration: up to 5 seconds, or until you parry 5 attacks — whichever comes first.
  • Follow-up: shortly after a successful parry, press Major Action (sweeping strike in front) or Minor Action (long-distance dash slash). Both apply Wither on hit.
  • Cooldown: 20s, and it costs 15 Energy.
  • Lock-on does not exist in Mistfall Hunter — aiming is manual.

No lock-on, no universal block — confirmed

Players searching "how to parry" usually expect a block/parry button like in souls-likes. In Mistfall Hunter there isn't one. In the Steam Community "Combat system" thread, the thread starter wrote: "I believe the game's combat system could have been more fun and interesting if there had been a blocking and parrying system… I also find it difficult to fight enemies since the character never locks onto the target."

Player reactions are split: some called the lack of block and lock-on "a weird choice," while others defended it — "Lack of lock on and parries is very refreshing… This is a skill based game." Either way, the takeaway is the same: don't go looking for a bindable block/parry key or an auto-lock — neither exists. Defensive play is handled by Dodge, movement and weapon skills like Greatsword's Parry.

How to use Parry

  1. Equip any Greatsword. Parry is slotted on the weapon and shared by every Greatsword you own.
  2. Activate the skill in combat to enter the defensive stance — you'll parry frontal attacks and recover Energy on each successful parry.
  3. Watch the timer: the stance lasts up to 5 seconds or until you've parried 5 attacks, whichever comes first.
  4. After a successful parry, follow up fast: press Major Action for a sweeping strike against enemies in front, or Minor Action for a long-distance dash slash. Both apply Wither.
  5. Mind the 20s cooldown — don't burn the stance on stray hits; save it for readable, telegraphed attacks (heavy swings, bosses, PvP engages).

Note: the exact keybinding for Major/Minor Action is your normal attack inputs after a parry — it's a timing follow-up, not a separate key. If a specific attack (e.g. ranged or magic projectiles) can be parried feels inconsistent in practice, treat that as pending in-game testing rather than a guarantee.

Best classes & weapons for Parry

Any class can slot Greatsword skills, but the Withered Knight is the dedicated Greatsword specialist (Heavy · Greatsword) — the database describes it as excelling at "toughness-break and parry against tough targets," and 38 of the game's recommended builds feature Parry. If you want a parry-focused playstyle, that's the class to try first.

Greatswords you can slot Parry on (by rarity): Wulfen Einherjar – Blade of Inquisition (Legendary), Sacred Heart Greatsword (Epic), Exile Greatsword (Excellent), Executioner Greatsword (Rare), Squire Greatsword (Common), Rusty Squire Greatsword (Damaged).

Combat tips without lock-on

  • Aim with your character's facing. Attacks land where your character faces — there's no auto-aim, so keep the camera/character aligned with your target.
  • Dodge is your universal defensive tool. Since there's no block button for most builds, spacing and dodge timing carry the defensive load.
  • Use Parry for telegraphed hits. Heavy, slow attacks are the best parry targets; don't stance up against rapid chip damage.
  • Ranged comfort: community players note ranged play (Blackarrow, Sorcerer) feels more comfortable without lock-on — you control the distance instead of chasing targets.
  • Expect a skill curve. The same players defending the design call it "a skill based game" — manual aim takes practice, especially in PvP.

FAQ

  • Is there a parry button in Mistfall Hunter? No universal one. Parry is a Greatsword skill you activate in combat — it's not a bindable key.
  • Can I lock onto enemies? No — the game has no lock-on. Aiming is manual (confirmed by players in the Steam Community combat thread).
  • Which class is best for parry? Withered Knight with a Greatsword — the class is built around heavy melee and parrying tough targets.
  • Does Parry work against ranged or magic attacks? The skill says "frontal attacks"; specific projectile behavior hasn't been fully documented — pending in-game testing.
  • Is Parry in the trio mode different? Yes — it has a shorter base cooldown in trio mode (per the skill database).
