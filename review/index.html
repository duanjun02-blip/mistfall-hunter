Mistfall Hunter Review — Is It Worth Playing?

The honest summary: Mistfall Hunter is hugely popular and genuinely divisive. It sold/shipped 1 million players in its first week, but Steam reviews sit at 62.55% Mixed and critics average 71/100 on Metacritic. Here's what both sides say, so you can decide.

So: not a critical darling, not a flop. It's a launch-week verdict that matches the live audience — big interest, real complaints. Treat every number as a snapshot; the game is being patched quickly.

What reviewers praise

  • Soulslike combat + extraction tension. GameSpot's critic wrote that Mistfall Hunter "made me a believer in extraction games" — the PvPvE stakes raise every fight.
  • Art and atmosphere. Dark fantasy with god-blood (Gyldenmist) corruption is consistently praised.
  • No pay-to-win. The devs explicitly market fair competition with no paid power; monetization is cosmetics.
  • Movement skill depth. No lock-on and ability-based mobility make duels read like fighting games (see our movement guide).

What players criticize

  • Balance. The most common complaint — creators like Soulgrot argue the tier list is "completely broken" right now.
  • Server/hotfix pain. Launch-week outages and rapid patches frustrated players.
  • No lock-on feel. Some players dislike the free-aim melee system; it's the game's biggest taste test.
  • Monetization optics. High-priced cosmetics (discussed around $1,000+ skin bundles) rubbed some players the wrong way — cosmetics only, but expensive.
  • Currency overload. Players joke about the "7 currencies" (Soul Coins, Fate Coins, Gyldenblod, Star Silver, Moonlight Nectar…). We break down the two most-searched in our Soul Coins and Moonlight Nectar guides.

How the devs are responding

Bellring Games has been unusually active in week one: rapid hotfix announcements, a Fair Play / anti-cheat enforcement update, and a roadmap published Aug 5, 2026 that promises single-player testing for Brandrgarde, new seasonal quests, monsters, matchmaking events, weapons, and a confirmed Season 2. The official stance on complaints is speed-of-response rather than silence.

Our take

Try it if: you like extraction games (Tarkov, Dark and Darker), Soulslike duels, or want a game with 30k+ concurrent players in week one. The core combat is the strongest argument and it's on sale at launch pricing right now.

Hold off if: you hate free-aim melee, grind-heavy currencies, or prefer fully polished launches. The balance and server complaints are real — they're also the kind of thing that changes fastest in the first month.
