Mistfall Hunter Withered Knight — Guide, Skills & Builds

The Withered Knight is Mistfall Hunter's Greatsword class: a slow, deliberate bruiser that marks enemies with Withering Sigils and detonates them with Reckoning. It also picks up a second stance, Polearm & Shield, in Season 1. This guide covers the mechanics, both Greatsword builds, the new stance and the latest patch changes.

What is the Withered Knight?

The Withered Knight is the newest of the six classes, added for the June 2026 Open Beta, and the game's heavy two-handed fighter. It carries a Greatsword with extended reach — that reach is central to how it controls space in a fight.

  • Role: tank / bruiser  ·  Weapons: Greatsword, then Polearm & Shield  ·  Difficulty: High  ·  Damage: Physical
  • Strengths: massive Wither debuff damage, super-armor charges, excellent in duels and team fights.
  • Weaknesses: slow and punishable if pressure lapses; one of the hardest classes to master.

It is the closest thing the game has to a tank, but it plays more like a bruiser: it wants to be in melee, mark priority targets and make those marks pay off. See our Classes overview and best class guide for how it compares to the other five.

Core mechanics: Sigils, Reckoning, Parry, Thorn Guide

  • Withering Sigils — mark enemies during combat to set up strategic advantages. The core Withering talent decides your style: grind enemies down with stacked sigils, or hold them to break a formation at the perfect moment.
  • Reckoning — the class's Execution mechanic. It detonates the sigils you placed, so timing matters more than raw button-mashing.
  • Parry — a precise-timing counter. In skilled hands it balances offense and defense effectively; it unlocks at an earlier level now and has a longer counter window than at launch.
  • Thorn Guide — grapples and drags enemies, giving the Knight a control role for the team. Its pull collision was fixed post-beta, so it connects more reliably.

Greatsword builds

The Greatsword splits into two playstyles, and the Withering talent you pick decides which one you lean into:

Skills and talents can be mixed across the Greatsword and Polearm & Shield branches, so a build is not locked to one stance's tree. See all class builds for full loadout examples.

Polearm & Shield (Season 1 stance)

The Greatsword was never meant to be the class's final form. The Withered Knight's second stance, Polearm & Shield, arrives with Season 1. It brings six new skills and — unusually — the ability to pull a downed teammate back up from a distance, which no other stance in the game offers.

Its talents and skills branch toward either damage or support, so the same class can be a front-line threat or the squad's safety net. The developers describe the intended rhythm as a stance dance: drag an opponent in with Thorn Guide, burst them down, and when they burn a mobility skill to escape, swap to the polearm, pin them with a thrown strike, and finish with a shield bash that breaks their guard.

The kite shield works differently from the Mercenary's sword and shield: you charge power while actively blocking, then cash it in on a counterattack. Several skills carry block protection that resists interruption, similar to Super Armor. The turn rate on some polearm skills was deliberately capped so the reach doesn't become oppressive. Two core talent paths split the stance:

  • Quick Shield Bash (aggressive vanguard) — raises the charging efficiency of the shield's heavy strikes. A shield-raise dodge builds charge quickly, letting you suppress with consecutive dashes and use the polearm's reach to break enemy lines.
  • Enhanced Shield Bash (team-oriented defence) — infuses the shield for an empowered heavy strike. Landing it on a charged shield triggers an AoE knockback that repels nearby enemies and pulls focus fire off your allies.

Balance history

The Greatsword was tuned after the June test, when the Withered Knight was the most-played class:

  • Parry now unlocks at an earlier level and its counter window is longer.
  • Thorn Guide collision problems that made the pull fail were fixed.
  • The second-stage branch of Breakthrough Charge lost its heavy knockback (it had been shoving ranged opponents out of range instead of keeping them in).
  • The core Legacy talent restores more health when the Knight downs a rival hunter; light and heavy strike hitboxes were widened slightly.
  • Endless Flow gives slightly less cooldown reduction than before, because the Polearm & Shield also benefits from it.

Aug 6, 2026 patch: the Energy cost of both Sprinting Slash and Withering Mark was reduced — a small buff after community calls for more. The same patch fixed skills catching on obstacles and launching the character into the air, casts being cut short by the Inspect action, and Thorn Guide being able to drag Richie the Merchant in the hub. Full details in our patch notes page.

How strong is it?

Multiple outlets rank the Greatsword branch S-tier when played well — the Wither + Reckoning loop is one of the highest-ceiling damage kits in the game. The catch is execution: it is slow, and a lapse in pressure is easily punished. It is not the pick for absolute beginners, but it is a top-tier dueler and team-fight anchor. Lore

The Withered Knights were once the glorious Rose Knights of the southern kingdom of Gaenaria. After falling from grace they were exiled to the North, where the bitter cold, years of war and a savage foreign land reshaped them. They kept their formal armor and chivalric code, and they work to find the missing Pontiff and reclaim their former glory. The Holy Sigil they bear — a vertical line piercing a circle — ties them to the legend of Saeria Sith, the incarnation of the Supreme Deity in Gaenarian faith.

FAQ

  • Is the Withered Knight good? Yes — its Greatsword is commonly ranked S-tier at high skill, but it's one of the harder classes to master.
  • How do I unlock it? It's one of the six playable classes; you can switch to it at Dew after the tutorial quest for a Gyldenblod fee.
  • When does Polearm & Shield arrive? With Season 1 — it's the class's second stance with six new skills and the only ranged revive in the game.
  • Greatsword or Polearm & Shield? Greatsword is the aggressive, high-damage option; Polearm & Shield leans into reach, guard breaking and supporting the team.
  • Did the Aug 6 patch change it? Yes — Sprinting Slash and Withering Mark cost less Energy, plus several bug fixes.
