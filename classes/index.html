All Mistfall Hunter Classes

Mistfall Hunter has six playable classes, each with two distinct weapons or playstyle branches. Here is everything you need to pick a starting class — roles, weapons, strengths, weaknesses and how class switching works.

Mercenary — Frontline Fighter

Weapons: Hammer · Sword & Shield  ·  Difficulty: Beginner friendly  ·  Damage: Physical

The Mercenary is the versatile melee fighter: durable, easy to learn and effective in both solo and team play. The Hammer path excels at heavy stagger pressure, gap-closing and AoE damage (Hammer Dash, Earthshaker). The Sword & Shield path is a balanced, defensive style built around blocking, perfect parries and counterattacks, with stacks that pay off in a big Stacked Slash.

Strengths: strong frontline presence, great survivability, reliable in solo and trio. Weaknesses: limited ranged pressure, vulnerable to fast/mobile enemies, heavy weapons punish poor timing. The Mercenary is the recommended starter class for most players.

Sorcerer — Ranged Burst Mage

Weapons: Staff (Elemental / Stardust)  ·  Difficulty: High  ·  Damage: Magic

The Sorcerer is a glass-cannon mage with a wide range of explosive firepower — Fire Bolt, Thunder Pierce, and the Starfall school — plus defensive tools like Forbidden Gate and Mistwalk. Its two branches are Elemental (fire/lightning pressure) and Stardust (burst damage). It is one of the most complex but most rewarding classes in the game.

Strengths: huge burst, zoning, strong in trios and PvE. Weaknesses: small health pool, punishing when skills are on cooldown, not recommended for solo beginners. See the Sorcerer build guide.

Blackarrow — Ranged DPS / Scout

Weapons: Bow · Javelin  ·  Difficulty: Easy–Medium  ·  Damage: Physical

The Blackarrow wields the Bow (and later the Javelin), starting fights from far away — often from high ground — with an arsenal of situational arrows and Shadow Step for repositioning. It is a great pick for players who like to outsmart and outposition opponents, and one of the strongest classes at launch in both trio and solo play.

Strengths: safe ranged DPS, excellent kiting, straightforward to play, strong in PvE and PvP. Weaknesses: needs good spacing; hard-countered by a good Seer or Withered Knight.

Aug 6 patch: Steel Arrow no longer knocks enemies back on uncharged shots, and fully charged shots knock back slightly less — a small nerf to Blackarrow’s control, not its damage.

Shadowstrix — Assassin (the "Rogue" class)

Weapons: Dagger · Dual Blades  ·  Difficulty: High  ·  Damage: Physical

The Shadowstrix is the rogue/assassin archetype: stealth, poison, crows and burst damage. Daggers are the closer/escape tool; Dual Blades are the main damage weapon, with skills like Crow Storm, Spinning Slash and Bloody Blade Dance. It is the strongest solo class in many tier lists thanks to invisibility — just remember that invisible Shadowstrix players give off a heartbeat sound.

Strengths: elite in PvP, can slip past enemies to loot and extract, ambush potential. Weaknesses: struggles in hard PvE boss fights, very low error tolerance. If you came looking for a "rogue build", this is your class.

Full Dual Blades build, talents, affixes and counters: → Shadowstrix (Rogue) build guide.

Seer — Support / Healer

Weapons: Catalyst · Mace  ·  Difficulty: Low mechanics, high game sense  ·  Damage: Magic

The Seer is the only true support class: healing, shields and runes like Punishment Rune and Binding Rune. If you prefer the Mace over the Catalyst you can embrace the aggressive Blasphemer branch instead. The Seer is a great trio pick (healing wins long fights) but is generally not recommended for solo beginners.

Strengths: best sustain and utility in the game, strong in trios. Weaknesses: low damage output, hard to pick up, healing is wasted in solo play unless you play Blasphemer.

Withered Knight — Tank / Bruiser

Weapons: Greatsword · Polearm & Shield  ·  Difficulty: High  ·  Damage: Physical

The Withered Knight is the closest thing to a tank, choosing between an aggressive Greatsword or a defensive Polearm & Shield. Skills like Breakthrough Charge, Radiant Retribution, Intervene and Sacred Bulwark let it protect allies and apply Wither debuffs. Several outlets rank its Greatsword branch S-tier when played well, but it is one of the harder classes to master.

Strengths: massive damage with Wither debuffs, super-armor charges, strong in PvP duels. Weaknesses: slow and punishable if pressure lapses; Polearm & Shield is currently considered weaker.

Aug 6 patch: Sprinting Slash and Withering Mark cost less Energy — a small buff for the Withered Knight after community calls for more. More adjustments are expected.

How to change classes

After the tutorial, finish the next quest and you will unlock the option to change classes by talking to Dew. You can switch freely, but keep these rules in mind:

  • Valuable items must be stored in your warehouse first.
  • There is a Gyldenblod fee that increases as you level up.
  • There is a cooldown before you can switch again — shorter at lower levels.
  • For a short window after switching, a Reversion lets you switch back for free.

There is also a practice ground at camp, so you can test classes without risking any loot.
